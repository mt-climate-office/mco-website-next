Most drought indices compare this year's weather with a fixed stretch of past years. As the climate shifts, that baseline drifts away from what is normal now. NGP RIDR is designing a drought early warning system for the Northern Great Plains that works from the water actually on the land instead.

The system's drought indicators come from the water balance, meaning soil moisture, groundwater, streamflow and evapotranspiration (the water plants and soil give off to the air). Machine learning estimates them from mesonet and soil moisture networks across the region.

The project is funded by the National Science Foundation's Regional Resilience Innovation Incubator (R2I2) program. Phase I is a two-year, $500,000 award to develop the concept and bring together the people, data and methods it needs. A successful Phase I leads to a proposal for a Phase II award of up to $15 million over five years, to run the system across Montana, North Dakota, South Dakota, Nebraska and Wyoming.

Kelsey Jencso, Montana's State Climatologist, directs NGP RIDR, and Kyle Bocinsky and Zach Hoylman from our office are co-investigators. The team includes the South Dakota and Nebraska state climatologists, the South Dakota Mesonet, NOAA's National Integrated Drought Information System, the Montana Department of Natural Resources and Conservation, the Bureau of Land Management, the USDA Agricultural Research Service and the FALCON Network of the American Indian Higher Education Consortium.
