NGP RIDR is funded by the National Science Foundation's Regional Resilience Innovation Incubator (R2I2) program. Phase I is a two-year, $500,000 award to develop the concept and bring together the people, data and methods it needs. A successful Phase I leads to a proposal for a Phase II award of up to $15 million over five years, to run the system across Montana, North Dakota, South Dakota, Nebraska and Wyoming.

The project is working on drought indicators drawn from the water balance (soil moisture, groundwater, streamflow and evapotranspiration), estimated from mesonet and soil moisture networks with machine learning. These would replace indices measured against fixed historical baselines.

Kelsey Jencso, Montana's State Climatologist, directs NGP RIDR. Kyle Bocinsky and Zach Hoylman of the Montana Climate Office are co-investigators. The team includes the South Dakota and Nebraska state climatologists, the South Dakota Mesonet, NOAA's National Integrated Drought Information System, the Montana Department of Natural Resources and Conservation, the Bureau of Land Management, the USDA Agricultural Research Service and the FALCON Network of the American Indian Higher Education Consortium.
