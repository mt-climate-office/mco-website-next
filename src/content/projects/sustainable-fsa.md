Sustainable FSA is a three-year research partnership between the Montana Climate Office and the USDA Office of the Chief Economist, the USDA Climate Hubs and the Farm Service Agency. It is funded through a cooperative agreement with the Office of the Chief Economist.

Eligibility for the Livestock Forage Disaster Program depends on each county's Normal Grazing Period and on the US Drought Monitor. The project rebuilt the Normal Grazing Period calculation from NOAA nClimGrid daily data on the 1991–2020 climate normals. The workflow can be re-run on each new normal.

Through Freedom of Information Act requests, the project obtained and archived the county Normal Grazing Periods for 2008–2026, the program's eligibility determinations, and the county boundaries used to make them. It also keeps weekly mirrors of the US Drought Monitor, back to January 2000.

Graduate students supported or advised by the project have completed four M.S. theses on how the program is delivered, including interviews with Farm Service Agency staff.

The work builds on [*Drought assessment has been outpaced by climate change*](/research/publications/hoylman-2022-drought-assessment/) (Hoylman, Bocinsky and Jencso, 2022). The project's reports, data archives and code are on its site.
